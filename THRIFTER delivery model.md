**THRIFTER** 

**So the viable model for Thrifter: hub-and-spoke, on batched delivery days.**

Here's the shape of it. You set an order cutoff and fixed delivery days — say, order by Wednesday midnight, delivered Thursday–Friday. When orders close, every seller with a sale gets notified: *drop your item at the hub by Thursday 10am.* Items get checked in, quickly inspected, and bundled into one labeled bag per buyer. Then 2–3 trusted boda riders (contracted per batch day, not employed (never build a fleet) run zone routes across the city: Ntinda/Kira Thursday, Rubaga/Makindye Friday, etc.

Batching is the whole trick. Thrift isn't urgent — nobody needs baggy jeans in 30 minutes — so buyers happily accept "delivered Thursday" if you say it upfront, and batching is what makes 10-seller consolidation affordable instead of ruinous. It also fits your drop-day culture perfectly: order window closes, delivery day lands, the week has a rhythm.

**The details that make or break it:**

* **Seller drop-off, not rider pickup, as the default.** Vinted's economics work because sellers do the shipping labor; yours should too. Incentivize drop-off with faster payout and a ranking boost; offer rider pickup only as a paid option. Put the hub downtown near Owino/Gazaland — most sellers pass through there anyway, so drop-off friction is nearly zero. The hub itself can be one room with shelves and labeled bags.  
* **Availability confirmation within hours.** The \#1 killer of multi-seller marketplaces is the seller who already sold the item offline. When an order lands, sellers confirm within 6–12 hours or the item auto-cancels and refunds. Track reliability; repeat no-shows get delisted.  
* **Never hold the bundle hostage.** If one of ten sellers misses the deadline, refund that item and ship the other nine on schedule. One flaky seller must never ruin the experience of the whole order.  
* **Prepayment only — no cash on delivery for consolidated orders.** With COD, you pay to collect from ten sellers and the buyer can just… not answer the door. Until your bank account lands, an MTN MoMo merchant code or Airtel Pay on a business line works: buyer pays, admin confirms, *then* collection begins. Aggregators like Pesapal or Flutterwave can also get you collecting before full banking is sorted. Hold funds and pay sellers after delivery confirmation — that escrow is also your trust story for buyers.  
* **One flat delivery fee, whatever the order.** "Ten shops, one delivery fee" is not just pricing — it's a marketing line. Add a free-delivery threshold later to push basket sizes up, since bigger baskets amortize your costs.
